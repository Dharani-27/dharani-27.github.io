About

I am a Ph.D. student at UMBC, where I work on making large language models trustworthy: whether their answers hold up, whether their reasoning can be inspected, and whether they behave safely when the input is adversarial. I am joining Prof. Manas Gaur’s group this year.

My earlier work is in acoustic sensing and security. With Dr. Dong Li, I built ultrasonic systems that verify whether a human, rather than an automated agent, is present at a device, and that watermark audio against voice scams. That work taught me to design experiments around a signal an attacker can manipulate, which is the habit I bring to evaluating language models.

Before UMBC I completed a B.Tech. in Computer Science at Vellore Institute of Technology, and an M.P.S. in Data Science at UMBC.

Research interests

  • Reliability and consistency of language models Whether a model gives the same answer to the same question asked differently, and what that instability means for the tasks people hand to it.
  • Explainability Explanations that reflect what a model actually did, rather than a plausible story written after the fact.
  • Safety under adversarial input How models fail when someone is trying to make them fail, and what evidence or structure makes them harder to mislead.
  • Security applications Language models as assistants in security work, including incident analysis, and the acoustic sensing and watermarking systems I have built.
