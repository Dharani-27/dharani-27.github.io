Research

My research is in trustworthy AI, focusing on consistency in multi-agent large language model (LLM) systems. I do this work in the KAI² Lab at UMBC with my advisor, Dr. Manas Gaur.

Current focus

Ongoing Ph.D. research

Consistency in multi-agent LLM systems

I approach this problem in three steps:

  1. 1

    Define

    What does “consistent” mean for a multi-agent system?

    Consistency is usually defined for one model and one prompt. I'm working on a definition that fits settings where many agents interact.

  2. 2

    Diagnose

    Where does inconsistency come from?

    I'm tracing inconsistent behavior back to its root causes, so that it can be understood and fixed rather than just observed.

  3. 3

    Measure

    How do we evaluate it?

    I'm working toward metrics and benchmarks that measure consistency in multi-agent settings, so different systems can be compared.

Results from this project will be posted on my publications page as they appear.

Earlier work

Before focusing on LLMs, I worked on security problems where trust between people and technology is at stake.

Securing human–AI interaction with ultrasound

As AI agents become able to act and speak for people, it matters whether a real person is present and whether a voice can be trusted. This work uses inaudible ultrasound signals on everyday devices to address both questions.

  • EchoGuard: continuous ultrasound-based verification that a human, not an AI agent, is present (EdgeSP 2026, co-first author).
  • Ultrasound Watermark: real-time acoustic watermarking against voice scams on smartphones (EdgeSP 2026).

Understanding malware evolution

MASCOT is a manually reviewed dataset of 6,032 malware source-code samples. It is used to study malware from a software-engineering perspective and to trace how code reuse drives malware lineages.

  • MASCOT (arXiv preprint, 2025).
